\section*{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\paragraph{Where the method is weak.}
Effects on video are small, and at sentence level OmniTrace reaches about 60\% of the lift of the perturbation baselines and a third of their specificity; its top-ranked second is human-marked evidence no more often than chance.
Summed attention favors large units (\Cref{sec:size}), and neither the sum nor the per-token mean is best on both models.
On MiniCPM-o~4.5 with image--text input, the human-marked quotes matter three times more than the quotes OmniTrace ranks first, so on that model the ranking misses the most important sources (\Cref{appendix:variants}).
The label threshold was checked on small development splits; on audio and video the causal criterion did not single out one value.

\paragraph{Scope.}
OmniTrace needs access to attention weights and does not apply to API-only models.
The examples and tests in this paper use reasoning-style responses of 6 to 10 sentences. Its resolution is the source unit: it finds which image and which second, not a region within an image or a word within a passage.
Video questions were run on frames without the audio track, so we do not study how a model divides its reliance between the sound and the picture of the same clip.
The baselines were run on one of the two models, and the learned baseline AT2 is not yet included.
All data are in English.

\paragraph{Evaluation.}
Hiding a unit changes the input, and a large drop shows that the response depends on those positions, not that a person would accept them as evidence.
Our random control matches the number of units, not their token count, which matters for image--text input where quote sizes differ.
For Qwen2.5-Omni, the teacher-forced replay in half precision does not reproduce the generated tokens exactly (the replayed top token agrees with the generated one at 81--88\% of the positions, against 97--98\% for MiniCPM-o~4.5; \Cref{appendix:replay}). All comparisons are made under the same replay, so differences between methods are unaffected, but absolute drops for that model should be read with this in mind.
Qwen2.5-Omni answers the image--text questions poorly (\Cref{tab:stats}); attribution there describes what the model relied on, including when its answer is wrong.
